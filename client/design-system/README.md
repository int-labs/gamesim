# Stratagem design system

`@stratagem/design-system`: the design system of the modular simulations (ET Prime, Product, Banking, Mall) and the Admin. It is built from the Figma file "Bank Simulation (Copy)" and replaces MUI screen by screen, without breaking the running apps.

It is built bottom-up:

1. Foundations: design tokens, the locked Tailwind theme, fonts, icons, motion, number formatting. **Done.**
2. Components: controls, forms, overlays, navigation and layout, the Admin shell set first. **In progress:** `Button` (the Figma btnPublish, matched pixel by pixel against its render, plus the yellow Start Round and red End Round tones, outline and text buttons, loading and success states), `LinkButton`, `IconButton`, `Spinner`, `Input`, `FormField`, `TextField`, `PasswordField`, `TextArea` (growing from four lines to ten, with a character count), `Alert`; the shell: `AppShell`, `Sidebar` (with `SidebarHeader`, `SidebarAccount`, `SidebarNav`, `SidebarSection`, `SidebarItem`, `SidebarSubItem`, `SidebarFooter`), `PageHeader`, `Breadcrumb`, toasts (`ToastProvider`, `useToast`, `createToaster`) and `Wordmark`, each measured against the Figma Admin and player frames; the Admin's data set: `DataTable` (sorting, paging and selection controlled from outside, keyboard-reachable row links, skeleton and empty states, a sticky header, a compact density) with `HeatCell`, `Pagination` (rows per page, then the pages), `Dialog` and `ConfirmDialog` (focus trap, Escape, Cancel first for destructive actions, typed-name confirmation) with `NoticeDialog` (one action the player takes in: Round Ended), `Select`, `Tabs`, `Checkbox`, `Card`, `Badge`, `EmptyState` and `Skeleton`; the list's menus: `Menu` (with `MenuItem`, `MenuLinkItem`, `MenuSubmenu`, `MenuRadioGroup` and `MenuRadioItem`, `MenuCheckboxItem`, `MenuGroup`, `MenuSeparator`) and `FilterChip`; a select to type into: `Combobox`; `Tooltip` and `TooltipProvider`, also naming the items of the collapsed sidebar, and `InfoTip` (an explanation that also opens on tap); the player's decision `NumberField` with `PreviousValue`, and the decision `Slider`; `Switch`; `Avatar` (with `initialsOf`); the simulation page's charts: `ChartCard` and `BarChart` (with `niceTicks`), and `StackedBarChart`; the charts beside a product's decisions, each on a `ChartPanel`: `HorizontalBarChart` (Account), `DonutChart` (Revenue) and `ComboChart` (the loans' charge-offs, with `evenTicks`); the statements under a product's card and on the report pages: `StatementTable` (Business Performance, the P&L, the Balance Sheet, the Cash Flow); the feedback slides: `SlideDeck` and `Slide`; dates: `DatePicker`, `DateRangePicker` and `Calendar`, with `yyyy-mm-dd` day helpers (`parseDay`, `toDay`, `today`, `laterDay`, `formatDay`); the player's `KpiBar`, with `KpiBarActions` and `EnergyPill`; the round's interstitial, `RoundStarting` (Figma's drawing by default); the decisions: `DecisionPanel` (with columns per sub-product), `DecisionRow`, `DecisionCell`, the read-only values in a decision's column (`ReadOnlyField` and `ReadOnlyValue`), the segment card's `DecisionTiles` and `DecisionTile` with the round's `Figures`, the campaigns' `BudgetField`, the Enterprise page's `InitiativeTable` and `InitiativeHistory`, the `ResourceChip` family and `Select`'s decision look.
3. Data display, game and admin building blocks.
4. Motion, interaction and celebration.

Browse it in Storybook: `npm run storybook` (port 6106).

## Using it in an app

1. Depend on it: `"@stratagem/design-system": "file:../../../../design-system"` from a sim client, `"file:../design-system"` from `admin/` (until the workspace arrives), with React deduplicated in Vite: `resolve: { dedupe: ["react", "react-dom"] }`. React 18.3 or 19 is a peer dependency. The package ships TypeScript sources, kept compatible with the strictest app settings (ES2020 library, no unused locals or parameters). Import icons from `@stratagem/design-system/icons` (all of lucide at the design system's version, plus its own glyphs), so the product uses one lucide version.
2. Replace `@import "tailwindcss"` in the app's CSS entry with the design system's stylesheet, and keep `@tailwindcss/vite` in the app's Vite config:

   ```css
   @import "@stratagem/design-system/styles.css";
   ```

   It brings the tokens, the Tailwind utilities they allow, the fonts, and a reset and base styles. It scans the design system's own sources; the app's sources are found automatically.
3. Mark where the design system owns the page with `data-ds-root`. The reset and base styles apply only inside it: put it on `<html>` in an app without MUI, and on the root of each migrated screen while MUI is still on the page, so legacy screens keep the browser defaults they were built on. The design system's popups (dialogs, menus, tooltips, selects, toasts) carry it on their portals, so they are styled wherever they open, a legacy screen included. The portals sit at the end of `<body>`: an app that scales itself with CSS `zoom` puts it on its root element, not on `<html>`, or each popup lands at the zoom's fraction of its anchor's distance from the window's top left corner.
4. Mount the providers once, at the root:

   ```tsx
   <MotionProvider reduceEffects={facilitatorReducedEffects}>
     <NumberFormatProvider currency={simulationType.currency}>
       <App />
     </NumberFormatProvider>
   </MotionProvider>
   ```

5. While MUI is present, give its styles their cascade layer, so design-system classes win without `!important`:

   ```tsx
   <StyledEngineProvider enableCssLayer>
     <GlobalStyles styles="@layer theme, base, mui, components, utilities;" />
   ```

6. Frame the screens with `AppShell` and mount the toasts once (the player passes `variant="player"`):

   ```tsx
   <ToastProvider>
     <AppShell
       mobileBrand={<Wordmark>intlabs.</Wordmark>}
       sidebar={
         <Sidebar>
           <SidebarHeader brand={<Wordmark>intlabs.</Wordmark>}>
             <SidebarAccount label={user.name} description={user.role} actionLabel="Change password" onClick={openChangePassword} />
           </SidebarHeader>
           <SidebarNav>
             <SidebarSection title="Simulation management">
               <SidebarItem icon={Boxes} label="Simulations" render={<Link to="/simulations" />} active={isSimulations} />
             </SidebarSection>
           </SidebarNav>
           <SidebarFooter>
             <SidebarItem icon={LogOut} label="Log Out" tone="danger" onClick={signOut} />
           </SidebarFooter>
         </Sidebar>
       }
     >
       <PageHeader title="Simulations" description="..." actions={<Button iconStart={Plus}>New Simulation</Button>} />
     </AppShell>
   </ToastProvider>
   ```

   A link item closes the small-screen menu when chosen (router links included); an item without `href` or `render` is a button and leaves it open. `<AppShell fullBleed>` drops the page padding for an embedded app frame (give the frame `min-h-0 flex-1 w-full border-0`).

   The player's pages put the KPI bar on top and give the same energy pill and Submit to the bar (from 1280 px) and to the top bar (below):

   ```tsx
   const actions = (
     <KpiBarActions loading={loading}>
       <EnergyPill value={energyLeft} over={isOverEnergy} refusing={Boolean(energyAttempt)} />
       <Button onClick={openFinalize} disabled={!canSubmit}>{t("Submit")}</Button>
     </KpiBarActions>
   );
   <AppShell sidebar={...} mobileBrand={<Wordmark size="player">{brandName}.</Wordmark>} mobileActions={actions} topBar={<KpiBar rows={rows} actions={actions} loading={loading} />}>
   ```

## The rules (enforced by lint and tests)

- **Only tokens exist.** The Tailwind theme is generated from the tokens with every default removed (`--*: initial`): `bg-red-500`, `p-13` or `shadow-2xl` generate nothing, and `no-unknown-classes` reports them. Arbitrary values (`w-[137px]`), arbitrary variants, the `!` modifier, bare numbers where tokens have names (`z-50`, `duration-300`) and alpha steps outside the scale fail lint. Component measurements are read as token variables, which lint checks against the tokens: `h-(--button-height-md)`.
- **No raw colours** in TypeScript or CSS. Code that cannot use a class or a variable (charts, canvas) reads `tokens.color`.
- **No MUI, emotion, framer-motion, clsx or tailwind-merge.** Motion comes from `motion/react` with `m` components (features load lazily); classes are joined with `cn` and variants built with `tv`, which know the token names.
- **Text uses roles**: `type-body`, `type-label`, `type-page-title` and the rest, or the `Text` and `Heading` components.
- **Motion respects people**: reduced motion, and the app's Reduce effects switch (`MotionProvider reduceEffects`, which also sets `data-motion="reduced"` for the `motion-reduce:` and `motion-safe:` variants).

## Tokens

Edit the JSON in `tokens/`, then run `npm run generate` and commit the source with the generated files (CI fails when they drift). A token's CSS variable follows its path: `color.surface.page` is `--color-surface-page` and creates `bg-surface-page`, `text-...` and so on; `component.sidebar.width` is `--sidebar-width` (a variable only). Palette colours are variables only: components use semantic colours.

Values come from the final Figma screens and the Preline tokens they resolve to (`doc/research/2026-09-23-v2-discovery/figma/08f-foundations.md`), never from Tailwind's defaults, whose colours differ.

An app can set a semantic token for part of its page, as the CSS variable it is. A form field's idle edge (Input, Select, TextArea, the date field, the checkbox's box) is its own token, `--color-line-field`, which follows `line` (Figma's gray 200) wherever the app leaves it alone. So fields beside gray 400 controls, as in a list toolbar next to its Filter button, can take the controls' edge, while the page's dividers and cards keep `line`:

```css
.list-toolbar {
  --color-line-field: var(--color-line-control);
}
```

## Open decisions for the owner

- **Contrast.** Figma's green 500, red 500 and pink text fall below WCAG AA (2.1:1, 3.4:1, 3.5:1 on the page). The text tokens use deeper shades of the same hues (green 700, red 700, the deep brand pink); fills and icons keep the Figma hues. White on the pink primary button (3.9:1), on the pink cost chip (3.6:1, pink at 85%) and on the red End Round button (3.7:1) stays as in Figma until the owner chooses: keep, deepen the colour, or enlarge the label.
- **Icon stroke.** Figma draws 1 px strokes at every size (`thin`, the default). `regular` (1.5 px) reads better on projectors and low-density screens.
- **Shell and table text contrast.** Also below AA in Figma, and shipped one shade deeper: the sidebar's group titles and the team card's second line (gray 400, 2.5:1 on white; now gray 500, 4.8:1), the page description (gray 500, 4.4:1 on the page; now gray 600, 6.9:1), Log Out (red 500, 3.8:1; now red 700, 6.5:1), inactive tab labels and empty-state messages (gray 400 and gray 800 at 40%, about 2.5:1; now gray 500), the table header labels, the rows-per-page value and the current page (gray 500 on slate, 3.9:1; now gray 600, 6.1:1), gray text on a table row, page or tab while it is hovered or striped in slate (4.4:1 on slate 100, 3.9:1 on slate 200; it turns gray 600), the toolbar's search placeholder and the "Active Filter:" label (gray 400; now gray 500).
- **White on the dialog's Delete** (red 500, 3.8:1) is the same question as white on End Round, and waits for the same decision.
- **Where the sidebar folds away.** Figma's tablet frame (1024 px) shows the top bar and the drawer, so the persistent sidebar starts at 1280 px. Today's apps keep it down to 600 px.
- **The player's sidebar on smaller laptops.** Figma's desktop is 1440 px with the sidebar open. From 1280 to about 1340 px, the open sidebar leaves the one-row KPI bar (the enterprise pages) too little room for its four figures beside the energy pill and Submit, and its first label is cut. The recommendation is that the player's sidebar start as the rail below 1440 px (Figma draws the rail variant), which the player migration can set.

## Deliberate differences from Figma

- The collapsed rail keeps the rows of the full sidebar. Figma's rail variant puts the player's round chip 12 px lower and the Admin's items 6 px closer, which would make the icons jump as the sidebar collapses; the collapse itself follows the prototype (300 ms, ease out) with nothing jumping.
- The open business unit's products fold away on the rail instead of leaving an empty guide line.
- Toasts that leave by themselves have no close button, as in Figma; a toast that stays (`duration: 0`) gets one. Every toast also closes with Escape once F6 moves focus to the toasts, and with a swipe.
- Figma's strokes sit inside their boxes without taking room; inputs, alerts, toasts, cards, the dialog's edge, the table's frame, the account chip and the sidebar edge draw theirs as inset rings or an overlay, and a table row's rule is balanced by a pixel at its top, so the padding matches Figma to the pixel.
- On a phone, the pagination's Previous and Next keep only their chevrons, so the pages fit on one line. Figma has no phone frame for the Admin lists.
- Tabs that do not fit (ten rounds on a phone) keep their Figma size and scroll sideways in their own strip, with the selected tab brought into view; Figma draws five rounds on a desktop card only.
- A select keeps the inputs' 47 px once a value is chosen (Figma's grows to 52), opens its list at the field's width, and marks the chosen option with a check, which Figma leaves unmarked.
- A menu marks the current sort or filter with a check, which Figma leaves unmarked; the button that opened it stays lavender while it is open, as the Figma Filter does. Figma draws a shadow only under a menu's first level, and so does the design system. A submenu opens once the pointer rests on its row, as native menus do, or at once with the arrow key, Enter or a tap.
- The whole filter chip removes its filter, not only its 12 px circled x, so the target is the chip's full 28 px.
- Tooltips have the chart tooltip's 8 px corners; Figma's KPI tooltip keeps Preline's 4 px, and one radius serves both. Longer text wraps at 280 px (Figma's are one line). The collapsed sidebar names its items and the account in tooltips beside the rail, as today's apps do; Figma draws no rail tooltips. Tooltips open on hover and keyboard focus and are read as the anchor's description; a tap on a touch screen does not open one, where MUI's opened on a long press.
- The chart cards follow Figma's Revenue Comparison (one blue bar per team, the teams along the bottom), where today's apps colour each team and add a legend. Axis values are round steps from zero, each on its grid line; Figma's drawing labels $600M to $3,000M over bars that start at zero. A loss goes below the zero line, a team without a value has no bar, and before any value the plot says so ("Round has not started") without axis values. Team names wrap to two lines and are cut; the tooltip has the whole name. Bars grow in and move to new values. The chart is one tab stop: the arrow keys move from team to team, showing each tooltip, and screen readers hear each team with its value.
- Figma draws no stacked bar chart for the Admin. StackedBarChart takes BarChart's axes and the details of Figma's stacked bars on the player's pages: the values inside the segments in SemiBold 14/20, and the legend under the chart, an 8 px dot before each series. Its bars are two fifths of a team's column, where BarChart's are three tenths, so the values fit; a value wider or taller than its segment is left to the tooltip. The values read at 4.5:1 or more: white on the indigos and the blue, gray 900 on pink, teal, yellow, violet and gray (white on pink would read 3.9:1). A round where every value is 0 keeps the teams and the zero line, and says so over the plot. A short caption can sit under each team (the deposit slides' interest rates), in indigo, named in the legend and read with the team; today's draws them as a separate row that does not line up with the bars. Today's slides meant to write the values in the bars but never did (the chart dropped them); `showValues={false}` keeps that look.
- The charts beside a product's decisions follow Figma's product pages (5204:98482, 2193:396973), each on the slate `ChartPanel` under its title in Poppins SemiBold 18, gray 900 (Figma sets Account in 20, and two of the three titles in black). Account is a `HorizontalBarChart`: the values in the segments, round values under the bar and its name before it, the axis counting accounts where Figma's writes dollars. The new accounts are indigo-soft, their legend's colour; Figma's bar draws them in a lighter tint, on which the white value would read 3.1:1. Revenue is a `DonutChart`: Figma's 141 px ring, the parts about 5 degrees apart, the legend beside it while the chart is 320 px wide or more (a long name wraps rather than taking the legend under the ring), the total in the middle in gray 800 over its caption in gray 600 (Figma's #414d55 and #696d6e taken to the nearest tokens; gray 500 reads 4.4:1 on the slate). Its ring and its legend share the parts' colours, where Figma's differ; the parts take the palette's colours in order unless given their own (Figma's legend draws the third in a light blue that reads 2.3:1 on the slate, where a part of a chart needs 3:1; the palette's blue stands in for it in the stories). The loans' charge-offs are a `ComboChart`: bars 2 px apart filling their columns and a line with its points on a second scale, over five grid lines both scales share, each counting in the smallest round step that reaches its largest value (Figma's 0 to 32% and 0 to 14,000 in four steps). Figma's legend names only the bars; the line is named too, and so can the gray bars be (`mutedLabel`), which Figma leaves to their colour. A series can have another name in the tooltip (`tipLabel`), as Figma's tooltip says "Customer Count" where its legend says "Number of customers". The line is 2 px, where Figma's 1 px barely reads as it crosses the bars. Short of room, every second label (or third) shows along the bottom; at Figma's width all twenty-one fit. The tooltips are the chart tooltip everywhere (the name in gray 300, then a dot and the value in SemiBold), where Figma's Account and Revenue tooltips write the value in Regular. Each chart is one tab stop: the arrow keys move from part to part, showing each tooltip, and screen readers hear every part, one at 0 too, which has no segment to see. Before the round's figures a chart keeps its frame and says so, in gray 600; Figma draws no empty chart.
- The statement tables follow Figma's Tables (904:131713): a 16 px frame with the default drop shadow, a slate 200 header and 44 px rows, the labels in the column that takes the rest and the figures in 164 px columns, totals and headings SemiBold on slate 100, a line under a parent 20 px further in and in italic, and a 22 px slate band between groups. Figma colours the change green or red by its sign, but a statement's costs, its ratio of bad loans and its cost to income are better when they fall, and the lines do not say which way is good; so the change stays gray (gray 600 on a total's slate) unless the game gives a line its `changeTone`. The header labels are gray 600, as the Admin table's. A label that wraps grows its row rather than being cut at 44 px. On a narrow screen the figures scroll sideways inside the frame while the labels stay in view; Figma draws the desktop only. Figma's Balance Sheet and Cash Flow headers set the last year over the change column; here every column is headed by its own name.
- The round's interstitial sets "New round is starting!" in Poppins Regular 48, where Figma uses Inter, which the design system leaves out of its screens; the drawing keeps Figma's own ink and blue as tokens. It holds the page's focus while it shows, as a dialog does, where Figma's overlay is only drawn over the page. A notice that ends something (Round Ended) takes its action on Escape too, so the player is never left on a page the notice has already changed; clicking outside leaves it open.
- Figma draws the slides viewer at one size: an 809 x 455 slide in a 1450 px frame. A slide is laid out at that size and scales up to fill the frame, or the screen in full screen, so it reads the same at every size and content that fits once fits everywhere; it never scales down, so browser zoom still enlarges its text, and the frame scrolls instead. Full screen, which today's deck has and Figma does not draw, is a quiet button in the slide's corner. Previous and Next line up with the slide's edges, where Figma's sit 20 px inside them, and their arrows are the buttons' usual 16 px (Figma draws 14 here). The arrow keys, Page Up, Page Down, Home and End move the deck; screen readers hear each new slide's number and title. A slide can reveal its content in steps, as today's league table does, a team at a time.
- Today's feedback revenue table colours each cell by its place in its row (green, lime, orange, red). A `HeatCell` puts the tone on a tile inside the cell instead, a shade lighter, so the table keeps its lines and the value reads at 9:1 or more; the bands and the scoring (`heatScores`: 0.5 for an even row) are today's. `density="compact"` (40 px rows, 12 px at the sides, 14/20 text) lets such a table fit a slide; Figma draws only the 52 px rows.
- The switch is Figma's (Preline's, drawn in a hidden Create Simulation row): 50 x 28, a 24 px white thumb on a small shadow, the label SemiBold 16/24 and 16 px away. Figma draws no "on" state, so on is the forms' brand pink, as the checked checkbox. Off, the track is gray 500 instead of Figma's gray 200, which the white thumb does not stand out from (1.2:1, where controls need 3:1).
- The decision number field is Figma's (2097:184137: 40 px, a brand indigo 40% edge, the value at the end, a currency's symbol at the start; the round's key figure in pink) with today's InputField rules to the letter: digits grouped as they are typed (react-number-format, as today), a value that fits kept at once, one that does not refused 300 ms after the last keystroke with a message naming the limit and a shake, and leaving it (or Enter) putting back the limit it broke. The messages are today's words; they write amounts in the field's own currency, where today's wrote "$" for every currency. The message pill takes the design system's danger tones. Screen readers hear the currency's name ("Thai Baht") and "percent" after the value. Two of today's slips are not kept: a value the page sets is only shown (rounded to the field's decimals), never written back or checked, where today's field wrote back the rounded value; and a value that arrives while the player types waits until they leave the field, where what they typed wins, where today's replaced their typing.
- "Previous:" under a decision is Figma's italic 14/20 in gray 600 (today's gray 400 reads 2.5:1 on the slate panel), and a round without a value says "None" rather than a dash.
- An explanation beside a label (a KPI's) is an InfoTip: it opens on hover as a tooltip, and a tap or a press keeps it open, since a tooltip does not open on a touch screen.
- Figma draws avatars as pictures only. Without a picture (today's Admin team cards have none), or while one fails to load, the avatar shows the name's initials on brand indigo, where today's apps show one letter on MUI's default blue: two letters, because one would make every "Team ..." a T, and a Thai name's initial skips a vowel written before its consonant.
- Figma draws no combobox. It is the form input with a clear button and the select's chevron, over the select's list. An option may carry a second line (a person's email), and typing matches the label, that line and keywords, ignoring case and accents. A long list shows its first fifty with a count of the rest, scrolls within about eight options, and darkens a highlighted row's second line to gray 600 (gray 500 reads 4.4:1 on its slate).
- Figma draws no date picker. The date field is the form select with a calendar icon; its calendar sits on the select list's panel, with 40 px days and the chosen day in indigo, as selection is elsewhere (white on indigo reads 13:1). The calendar's behaviour comes from react-day-picker; every word goes through `Intl`, so the app's locale names the months and days and sets the week's first day.
- The KPI bar follows Figma's three frames: the glass bar from 1280 px (Navbar Variant2), the tablet's rows of chips from 1024 px, and the phone's Winning Metrics below. Figma has no frame between 440 and 1024 px, where four chips no longer fit a row: they stay folded under Winning Metrics, two to a line from 640 px. Winning Metrics opens its chips inside the bar, which pushes the page down rather than covering it.
- In the two-row bar each figure keeps its own width and the rows spread them apart, at least 16 px (Figma's fixed widths leave 27 px at 1172 px); short of room, a label wraps between words, as today's does. The one-row bar is Figma's: the figures touch and the first fills the row; short of room, its label takes a second line, then an ellipsis. Values and changes always show whole.
- A change badge is always 52 px, so the values line up (Figma's chips hug the green ones and fill the red ones), and a figure without a change says N/A in the zero badge's gray, which Figma does not draw.
- The info icon is gray 500 (4.4:1 on the chips' slate), where Figma draws gray 900 at 32% on the desktop bar and gray 400 on the chips (about 2:1, where a control needs 3:1). Its 24 px target reaches over the gaps, so the glyph sits Figma's 4 px after the label.
- The energy pill turns red while the decisions cost more energy than is left or a move is refused, and a refusal also nudges it with a red glow, as today's does; Figma draws neither.
